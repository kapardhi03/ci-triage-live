# Interview 13 — the handoff

Three questions: can another engineer reproduce the evidence, find every decision and its
cost, and continue from the known unknowns?

## Can they reproduce the evidence?

**Mostly yes, with two named gaps.**

**What reproduces cleanly.** `scripts/fetch_raw.sh` pins md5s for the three CSVs, so the
input is byte-identical or the pipeline stops. `uv run pytest` runs 115 tests including the
nine instruments. Every number in `artifacts/results/` was produced by a command in the
repository, and `run_id` hashes split, estimator, feature list, label, seed and data digest
so two results with equal ids and unequal numbers is a detectable bug.

**Gap 1 — the archives are not checksummed.** `scripts/fetch_archives.sh` verifies only that
each file is a readable gzip tar, because Zenodo publishes no per-file md5 for them. A
re-download that differs would not be caught. Stated in the script itself.

**Gap 2 — some invariants grade recorded artifacts, not live re-runs.**
`test_05_gate_deterministic_set_matches_ground_truth` asserts Jaccard 1.000 from
`infra.json`. A mis-parse that regenerated the artifact would be caught; one that left it
stale would not. The full re-run takes ~55 s and is not wired into the suite.

## Can they find every decision and its cost?

**Yes, and the costs are the part that is actually complete.**

`DECISIONS.md` indexes eleven decisions with what each gave up — a git-history dependency
accepted then cancelled, 0.069 AUC surrendered to report the deployment split, 300+ projects
of IDoFT forfeited on licensing. `EXPERIMENTS.md` indexes hypotheses with verdicts, including
the refuted ones.

Every phase has an `ai-ledger/` entry with one AI proposal rejected or narrowed **and the
reason**, so the counterfactual is inspectable rather than lost.

**The honest bit:** several decisions are recorded as *delegated* rather than *reasoned* —
phase 04's rejection of "drop all 8 history columns" carries no stated why, and the ledger
says so, so a reader knows how much weight it carries.

## Can they continue from the known unknowns?

**Yes — `KNOWNS.md` has 185 established rows and 58 open ones**, and the open column is the
one to work from. The five that matter are listed at the bottom of that file. Two of them
have named next steps and no implementation:

- **`cause_group` should be derived, not typed.** `docs/prior-work.md` identifies the
  double-fault measure and Q statistic as the standard instruments, computable from outputs
  already stored, and records that they were not applied. This also closes the gap where no
  coupling involving observer 2 was ever computed.
- **The `ExecutionTime` leak is decidable with information this dataset lacks** — whether it
  was measured on runs including failures. That is a question for the dataset authors, not a
  computation.

## What a new engineer must not conclude

**That the contract working means the observers work.** Phase 10 built a correct integration
contract; phase 11 measured it operating over two voices of which one is inert. The machinery
is sound and it has almost nothing to operate on. `docs/architecture.md` says this in its
last section deliberately, so nobody reads the diagram as evidence.

**That "no fine-tune" is a general claim.** It is scoped to 19 distinct messages on one
project. A corpus with genuine lexical variety could flip it.

## The strongest critique of the handoff, and the response

**Critique:** "You are handing over a system that does not work, with 58 open questions and a
shipped component that is a dictionary lookup. The honest summary is *we spent thirteen
phases establishing that this approach fails on this data*. Why would anyone continue?"

**Largely conceded, and the last clause is the part I would push back on.**

Conceded: three observers were matched or beaten by free alternatives, fusion added nothing,
the fine-tune was gated out, and the shipped component abstains on 17.8% of cases.

What makes it worth continuing is that the negative results are **load-bearing and probably
correct**. arXiv 2607.09345 independently reproduces four of them on different detectors and
a different dataset, and names the cause this project only reached as a symptom: *flakiness
is not a static property of test code.* A project that had reported 0.7569 and a working
three-observer ensemble would have produced a system that failed silently in production — and
`FAILURES.md` 1.1 predicts exactly that quotation as the first way this system will fool
someone.

The instruments are the transferable part. Nine invariants, each mutation-tested, guarding
the nine slices that would otherwise emit a plausible number while broken. Those outlive
every model in the repository, and three of them exist because the corresponding failure
already happened here and **no passing test caught it**.
