# Interview 07 — the review

## What calibration changed

Not what was predicted. The prediction was "ECE much better, AUC unchanged," on the
reasoning that a monotonic map preserves ranking.

AUC was unchanged for sigmoid (+0.0016) and moved for isotonic (−0.0116). Both are
monotonic, so the stated mechanism cannot explain a 0.013 spread between them: isotonic's
ties move the ranking, and `CalibratedClassifierCV` refits the base estimator on internal
folds, making the calibrated object an ensemble rather than a rescaled model. Direction
right for one method, mechanism wrong for both.

ECE improved by 6.6% (equal-width) and 2.5% (equal-frequency) — real, not "much better."

**What actually moved was cost-weighted risk: 0.5440 → 0.2708, a 50% drop.** The
ML-standard metrics barely twitched and the metric derived from the organisation's own cost
table halved. That number was only visible because the inverted cost mapping was caught and
fixed earlier in the same phase.

## What question the pivot answers

The pivot was: stop asking this observer to classify every red build; abstain where the
project is distributionally alien, and report risk–coverage.

It answers *"can this observer be useful on the subset where it is entitled to speak?"*

**The answer is no, and the reason is economic rather than statistical.** Abstention costs
1.5h on every covered row; the model's errors cost 0.0957 h/row. An oracle qualifier saves
2.7%. There is no subset worth carving out.

## Which claim the new scope no longer supports

**The claim that a tabular observer contributes to this system at all, on its own.**

Before this phase it was reasonable to say "we have a weak component that ranks at 0.68 and
fusion will make use of it." After it, the defensible statement is narrower: *this observer
ranks flaky above non-flaky at AUC 0.678 and cannot beat a constant policy at any
threshold.* It may still contribute **in combination** — that is phase 11's question, and it
is now open rather than assumed.

Three specific claims are dead:

1. **"A stronger model will help."** GBT scored 0.6781 against logistic regression's 0.6878.
   It is worse.
2. **"Abstention buys us the confident subset."** Ceiling 2.7%, oracle abstains on one
   project.
3. **"The observer is weak but directionally useful."** Its ten most confident predictions
   are 0/10 correct. Precision peaks at 10% against a 93% requirement.

## The strongest critique of a reported number, and the response

**Critique:** "AUC 0.6781 is not a real number. Remove one feature — `ExecutionTime` — and
it falls to 0.5577, barely above chance. You do not have a 13-feature observer, you have one
feature and twelve passengers. And that feature is your own pre-registered leak suspect, so
the headline may be measuring contamination."

**Response, and it is a partial concession.** The dependence is confirmed: 0.6765 → 0.5577
without it, so the observer is effectively one column. The leak question is *not* resolved
by that test, in either direction — a leak uniformly present in training and test folds
transfers perfectly well, so strong cross-project contribution is consistent with both
"real signal" and "uniform contamination." The decisive evidence is whether `ExecutionTime`
was measured on runs including failures, which the dataset does not ship.

What survives the critique is the phase's conclusion, because it does not depend on
resolving it. The observer fails to beat a constant **whether or not** `ExecutionTime` is
honest. If it is contaminated, the true result is worse, not better.

What the critique correctly damages is the *forward* claim. Phase 10 asks whether observers
are independent, and an observer that reduces to one column sets a harder test than
expected: three observers are three observers only if they do not all rest on the same
signal.
