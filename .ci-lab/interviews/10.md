# Interview 10 — the review

## The two questions

**Run level:** *is this test's failure flaky, a real defect, or infrastructure?*
Consumed by the engineer triaging a specific test, the quarantine list, and the flaky-test
backlog. One answer per test per build.

**Case level:** *does the 09:00 release ship?*
Consumed by the on-call release engineer at 02:47. One answer per build.

They are not the same question at different granularity. They have different consumers and
different artefacts, and `PROBLEM.md` only ever asked the second one.

## The observed input overlap

The literal intersection of the three observers' `Reads` is **empty** — computed by
`input_overlap`, not claimed. Observer 1 reads 13 static per-test features, observer 2 reads
the ordered run outcomes, observer 3 reads the failure text. No shared column.

And that check passing is why the real problem was nearly invisible:

```
ExecutionTime predicting "does observer 3 see the SSL NoSuchMethodError"
    AUC 0.6987   p = 1.06e-06

observer 1 predicting the label it exists to predict (LOPO)
    AUC 0.6765
```

**Observer 1's dominant feature predicts observer 3's input better than it predicts its own
target.** Zero shared columns, one shared physical cause — a JVM/SSL incompatibility that
produces both the long execution times observer 1 leans on and the `NoSuchMethodError` text
observer 3 retrieves.

## How the contract prevents agreement from being mistaken for evidence

Every `Evidence` record carries a `cause_group`, and records sharing one are collapsed to a
single voice **before** aggregation. Observer 1 and observer 3 share a group, so their
agreement contributes once. Adding further correlated records changes nothing —
`test_agreement_from_a_shared_cause_cannot_inflate_confidence` asserts that five correlated
records produce the same `p_real` as two, with `voices == 1` and `deduplicated == 4`.

Collapsing rather than averaging is the load-bearing choice. Averaging two correlated records
still lets one event contribute twice to the confidence; only dropping the duplicates stops
it.

The rule is mutation-tested. Removing de-duplication, or replacing the case-level rule with a
mean or a majority vote, each fails the suite.

## The strongest critique, and the response

**Critique:** "`cause_group` is a hand-assigned string. You measured one correlation, decided
observers 1 and 3 share a cause, and typed the same label onto both. The contract does not
*detect* correlated evidence — it enforces a grouping a human guessed. Observers 2 and 3
might share a cause nobody measured, and the contract would happily count them twice."

**Conceded, in full.** The de-duplication is only as good as the grouping, the grouping came
from one measurement on one project, and no pairwise coupling was computed for observer 2 at
all. A contract that enforced measured correlations — recomputing groupings from observed
output covariance — would be strictly better and does not exist here.

What the current design does buy: the grouping is **explicit, named in every record, and
frozen as a constant in the tests**. If the SSL incompatibility is fixed and the coupling
disappears, `SHARED_CAUSE_AUC` fails and forces the conversation. A system that inferred
groupings silently would drift without anyone noticing; this one cannot drift without a test
going red. That is weaker than detection and stronger than a principle.

## The claim this phase does not support

That the system works. Three observers exist and three were matched or beaten by a free
alternative. The contract makes their combination *meaningful*; it does not make it
*valuable*. Phase 11 measures fusion, and it starts from components whose individual
contribution is unproven — which is the honest position to start from.
